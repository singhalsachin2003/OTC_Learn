# Perpetual Swap

*A future with no expiry, held to spot by a payment*

A perpetual swap tracks the price of an underlying without ever expiring. Nothing settles it, so it needs another mechanism to stop it drifting away from spot: a funding payment exchanged periodically between longs and shorts, whose sign depends on which side the contract is trading. It is the clearest example in modern markets of a contract kept honest by an incentive rather than by a delivery date.

## Key terms

- **Funding rate** — The periodic payment between longs and shorts that keeps a perpetual contract close to the underlying index.
- **Funding interval** — How often that payment is exchanged — commonly every eight hours, which compounds quickly.
- **Index price** — A composite of external spot sources used as the reference, rather than the venue’s own last trade.
- **Mark price** — The valuation used for margin and liquidation, built from the index so a single order cannot move it.
- **Maintenance margin** — The equity level below which a position is closed out by the venue rather than allowed to go negative.
- **Insurance fund** — A pool absorbing the shortfall when a liquidation cannot be completed at better than the bankruptcy price.

## In practice

Perpetuals are the highest-volume derivative in digital asset markets, and the funding rate is watched as a positioning indicator in its own right. The design is now being borrowed elsewhere: the question it answers — how do you anchor a never-settling contract to a reference price — applies to anything continuously traded.
