# DV01 and Rate Risk

*What one basis point is worth*

DV01 is the change in the value of a position for a one basis point move in rates. It is how a rates desk states its risk, sets its limits and sizes its hedges, because it converts every instrument — a bond, a swap, a future, a whole book — into the same unit: money per basis point. Everything else in rates risk is a refinement of it.

## Key terms

- **DV01** — The change in a position’s value for a one basis point parallel move in rates.
- **PV01** — The value of a basis point on the par rate — the discounted annuity — rather than on the yield.
- **Modified duration** — The percentage change in price per unit change in yield, from which DV01 is derived.
- **Key rate DV01** — Risk reported bucket by bucket along the curve, rather than as one parallel-shift number.
- **Bump and revalue** — Computing sensitivity by shifting an input, repricing the book and taking the difference.
- **Convexity** — The second-order effect that makes DV01 inaccurate for large moves in either direction.

## In practice

Every rates desk runs a DV01 report by currency and by curve bucket, and the limit framework is written in it. The measure crosses into other desks too: a credit trader thinks in CS01, an FX forwards trader in the DV01 of the two currency legs, and a treasury function in the DV01 of the whole balance sheet.
